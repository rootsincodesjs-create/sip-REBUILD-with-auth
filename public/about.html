The Purpose of My SIP

When I was deciding on my SIP (Senior Integrated Project), I wanted it to be an academic story that documents everything I have learned at Kalamazoo College. I chose to major in Computer Science my freshman year, but I never imagined I would one day be building a webpage dedicated to Women of Color in STEM. Through this project, I wanted not only to show my growth in coding, but also to reflect my passion for Political Science and Critical Ethnics Studies. Sharing that passion allows me to help others understand the world around them. I hope this webpage inspires people to learn about marginalized and oppressed communities while creating a space to hear their voices and experiences.

As a first-generation Mexican American, I carry the stories of my family. I am deeply aware of the histories my parents brought with them when they migrated to the United States—never fully realizing how much power they gave me simply by passing on an identity and a culture rooted in our ancestral indigeneity. Before college, my dad often spoke about his journey from Toluca, and my mom about her hometown of Huetamo, Michoacán—two immigrants whose stories are filled with courage, hope, and purpose, from Mexico to the center of Texas. Their experiences share a common thread: the belief in “hay que seguir adelante.” They taught me that I am a Mexican American woman with challenges ahead of her, though I didn’t yet grasp the full weight of what that meant. Being Indigenous, being Brown, being a woman of color means living in a society that continues to oppress and diminish our identities. Political science gave me the language to understand that our identities can also be a source of strength—to take pride in who we are, to flourish, and to transform oppression into resilience.

For me, this project is a reflection of that journey. I am my parents’ first child to attend a four-year college, to major in computer science, to study abroad in Brazil, and now to stand on the edge of graduation. In many ways, I am living my parents’ dreams and wishes, carrying their story as part of my own. At the same time, this is my story—my identity, my journey, my voice. Studying abroad in Brazil ignited a fire that will not be diminished by oppression. I understand now how everything is connected and intentional, shaped by histories of colonization and resistance. That realization transformed me and continues to guide me as I finish this chapter—and, like my parents say, “hay que seguir adelante.”

Going to Brazil let me experience firsthand the realities I had only read about in political science courses. Discussing theory in class was one thing; standing in places like Salvador and São Paulo—where culture and identity are honored and fiercely protected—made it real and empowering. I was in a place where so much of Brazil’s history began, where the intertwined histories of oppression and resistance shaped society and culture. I stood among descendants of people who fought to sustain their Indigenous and Afro-Brazilian roots—who defended quilombos, built favelas into communities of survival and creativity, claimed freedom and identity, and who continue resisting today.

I also came to see that feeling “too Mexican” in the United States and “too American” in Mexico is not just a personal struggle—it is a product of colonialism, a system that tries to divide and diminish Indigenous people. Realizing this taught me that my identity holds power. I am not only living my parents’ dreams; I am continuing the stories of my ancestors and carrying their strength forward. This webpage is my way of honoring that—creating a space where Indigenous people, and women of color more broadly, can fight for their place, their culture, and their futures.

It moves me to know that we are still here—still resisting, still telling our stories. We carry the faces of our ancestors, and through us, they continue. My SIP is not only about computer science or political science; it is also about my art—my way of expressing the beauty, complexity, and resilience of my identity.
