Hello and welcome to my web page. My name is Sarah Jaimes Santos — a first-generation Mexican American college student, digital creator, and advocate for equity in STEM. As an indigenous woman in tech, I’ve often searched for faces like mine within my STEM spaces—this project helps change that. Our stories in STEM are powerful—and overdue to be seen. I created this space to shed light on the vital yet often overlooked contributions of women of color in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics. This platform is more than just a digital archive — it’s a growing community space. A place where our stories are seen, our voices are heard, and our journeys are celebrated. My goal is to empower women of color, past and present, by honoring their achievements, acknowledging their struggles, and building connections across generations. Whether you’re here to explore the timeline of pioneers, read the stories of students and professionals, or share your own — you belong here. Together, we’re redefining what representation in STEM looks like.

Powerful Women of Color in History

Photo by NASA, licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0 . Source.

Katherine Johnson is a trailblazing mathematician who broke barriers as the first African American to earn a Ph.D. in mathematics from the University of Connecticut. Like many women of color in STEM, she navigated spaces where she was often the only one who looked like her. Despite systemic barriers, she rose to become the Director of the Atlanta University Center Data Science Initiative, where she champions inclusive and socially conscious applications of math. Her journey is one of resilience and leadership, showing young Black women that their place in STEM is powerful, necessary, and transformational.

Resources & Solidarity

Being a woman of color in STEM shouldn’t mean having to be perfect every second just to earn a place. Yet too often, the expectation is that we prove ourselves again and again in male-dominated spaces. This constant pressure can feel isolating, but it also shows why building community matters.

By uplifting each other’s voices and normalizing our presence in these fields, we shift the narrative away from survival and toward belonging. Women of color shouldn’t have to fight for our spot—we should already be seen as an integral part of STEM. Community allows us to create that reality together: where our successes are celebrated, our challenges are shared, and our contributions are recognized without question.

Immigration

Immigration isn’t something new — it’s been happening for generations. Families have always moved, struggled, and rebuilt, often out of necessity, often against impossible odds. Too many times, though, the conversation turns people into statistics or problems, instead of seeing us as human beings with dreams, families, and futures.
